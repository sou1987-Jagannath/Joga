## Welcome — What Is This Guide?

Microsoft 365 Copilot is now available to you in Outlook, Word, and Excel. It can draft emails, summarise documents, write formulas, and a lot more — but only if you ask it the right way.

This guide gives you a simple formula to write good prompts, plus ready-to-use examples you can copy and adapt today. No technical knowledge needed.

---

## The One Formula to Remember

Good prompts have four ingredients. You don't always need all four, but the more you include, the better the answer.

| Ingredient | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | What do you want Copilot to do? | *"Summarise this report..."* |
| **Why / Who for** | A bit of background helps | *"...it's for a monthly team update..."* |
| **How long / What style** | Tell it the format you want | *"...as five bullet points, friendly tone."* |
| **Any extra rules** | Anything it should avoid or include | *"...don't use jargon."* |

**Easy way to remember it: think of it like briefing a helpful colleague.**  
You wouldn't just say *"write something"* — you'd say *"can you write a short summary of this for the team meeting, nothing too formal, just the key points."* Copilot works the same way.

You don't need all four every time. Even just being specific about *what* you want and *how long* it should be will get you a much better result than a one-word request.

## Copilot in Outlook

Copilot in Outlook can draft emails, summarise long threads, suggest replies, and help you write more clearly. Find it in the Copilot icon in the toolbar when composing an email or inside a message thread.

### Example Prompts

**Prompt 1 — Summarise a long email thread**
> *"Summarise this email thread in plain English. List the key decisions made, any outstanding actions, and who is responsible for each one."*

Use this when you've been added to a thread halfway through and need to catch up quickly, or before a meeting where a long email chain is the background.

---

**Prompt 2 — Draft a chaser email without sounding pushy**
> *"Draft a polite follow-up email to a supplier who hasn't responded to my invoice query from last week. Keep it professional and friendly. Two short paragraphs, no bullet points."*

Copilot will write the email for you. Review it, personalise the name, and send — or ask it to adjust the tone if it's not quite right.

---

**Prompt 3 — Rewrite an email to be clearer and shorter**
> *"Rewrite the email below so it's shorter, clearer, and easier to read. Remove any unnecessary sentences. Keep the main point and make sure it's still clear what I'm asking the person to do. [paste your draft below]"*

Paste your rough draft and Copilot will tighten it up. This is great for long-winded emails you've written in a hurry or messages that cover too many topics at once.

## Copilot in Word

Copilot in Word can draft documents, rewrite sections, summarise content, and help you structure your thinking. To open it, look for the **Copilot button in the Home tab** along the top ribbon — it has the same coloured swirl icon you'll see across all Microsoft 365 apps. Click it and a chat panel will open on the right side of the screen where you can type your request.

### Example Prompts

**Prompt 1 — Draft a section from scratch**
> *"Write a short executive summary for a quarterly finance update. The key points are: revenue was 4% above target, operating costs increased due to a one-off IT project, and the team is on track for year-end. Formal tone, three short paragraphs, no more than 200 words."*

Give Copilot the raw facts and tell it the format. You'll get a solid first draft to edit rather than starting from a blank page.

---

**Prompt 2 — Summarise a long document you've been given**
> *"Summarise this document. What are the three most important things I need to know? What, if anything, is being asked of me? Use plain English and bullet points."*

Use this when you've been sent a lengthy policy, report, or guidance document and need the key points without reading it end to end.

---

**Prompt 3 — Turn rough notes into a structured document**
> *"I've written some rough meeting notes below. Please turn them into a clear set of meeting minutes with: date and attendees at the top, a summary of what was discussed, and a numbered action list at the bottom with owner names. [paste your notes below]"*

Works brilliantly after a meeting where you've jotted down bullet points but need something shareable with the wider team.

## Copilot in Excel

Copilot in Excel can explain what a spreadsheet contains, suggest formulas, highlight patterns, and help you analyse data without needing to know advanced Excel. Find it via the **Copilot button in the Home tab** along the top ribbon.

> **Quick tip:** Copilot works best when your data has column headers in the first row (e.g. Date, Department, Amount) and no completely blank rows in the middle. If your spreadsheet already looks like a normal data table, you're good to go — no changes needed.

### Example Prompts

**Prompt 1 — Understand what's in a spreadsheet you've inherited**
> *"What does this spreadsheet contain? Give me a plain English summary of what each column represents and what the data seems to be tracking."*

Useful when you've been handed a file by a colleague and need to get up to speed without asking them to explain every column.

---

**Prompt 2 — Find patterns or outliers in your data**
> *"Look at the spend figures in this spreadsheet. Are there any months or entries that stand out as unusually high or low compared to the others? Point them out and explain why they might be worth looking at."*

You don't need to know which column the data is in — Copilot can see the whole spreadsheet. Instead of scanning hundreds of rows manually, let it flag what's worth looking at. Always verify its findings before acting on them.

---

**Prompt 3 — Help with a formula you're not sure how to write**
> *"I want to add up the spend figures, but only for rows where the department is 'Finance' and the amount is over 5,000. What formula should I use? Please explain it in plain English so I understand how it works."*

Describe what you want to *achieve* rather than trying to use Excel terminology. Copilot will suggest the formula and explain each part in plain English — no need to Google it separately.

## Tips for Getting Better Results

**Be specific, not vague.**  
Instead of: *"Write me an email"*  
Try: *"Write a two-paragraph email to a client explaining that their invoice query has been passed to the payments team and will be resolved within 5 working days."*

**Tell it the tone and length you want.**  
Copilot doesn't know whether you want formal or friendly, a paragraph or a page. Say it upfront: *"keep it brief"*, *"professional but approachable"*, *"no longer than 150 words"*.

**If the first answer isn't right, ask it to adjust — don't start again.**  
You can say: *"That's good but too formal — make it friendlier"* or *"Shorten this by half"* or *"Add a bullet point for the deadline"*. Copilot remembers the conversation context.

**Treat the output as a first draft, not a finished product.**  
Always read what Copilot produces before you send, share, or act on it. It can occasionally get things wrong or miss some context. Think of it like a first draft from a helpful colleague — you'd still give it a quick read before forwarding it on. That's all that's needed.

## A Word on Data Safety — Please Read This

> This section connects to the **FinBridge Personal AI Usage Charter** covered in Week 1 of this programme. The same rule applies to Microsoft 365 Copilot — and to any other AI tool you might use.

**Microsoft 365 Copilot is safe to use with your work content** — it is connected to your own FinBridge Microsoft 365 account and operates within our company's secure environment. Your prompts and documents stay within FinBridge's system and are not used to train any external AI model.

**However, other AI tools are not the same.**

Tools like ChatGPT, Google Gemini, or any AI tool accessed through a personal browser tab are **external services**. Anything you type into them leaves our environment and may be stored, processed, or used by a third party.

**The rule is simple:**

> **Do not paste client names, account numbers, salary figures, contract values, financial forecasts, or any personal data into an external AI tool — ever.**

This means: if the information would be sensitive in an email to someone outside FinBridge, it is too sensitive for an external AI tool. That includes:
- Client names and contact details
- Any figures from payroll, board packs, or M&A documents
- Customer account references or transaction data
- Colleague personal details

**If you ever want to use an external tool for something general** (getting ideas, checking grammar on non-sensitive text), you can swap out any real details for made-up ones first. For example, instead of *"Summarise the contract terms for Acme Corp at £250,000 over 3 years"*, write *"Summarise a contract for a generic client over 3 years."* Get the structure you need, then fill in the real details yourself in Microsoft 365.

For anything work-related that involves real client or company information, stick to Microsoft 365 Copilot — it's what it's there for, and it's safe to use.

Not sure whether something is okay to type into a tool? Drop a message to IT — there's no such thing as a silly question on this one.
